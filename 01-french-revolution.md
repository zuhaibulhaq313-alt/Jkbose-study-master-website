# History — The French Revolution
## Background
Before 1789, French society was commonly described in three estates: clergy, nobility and the Third Estate (including peasants, workers and the middle class). Privileges and unequal taxation created resentment. The monarchy faced financial crisis, food shortages and rising bread prices; Enlightenment ideas challenged absolute rule and inherited privilege.

## Key events
- 1789: Estates-General met; representatives of the Third Estate asserted political authority and formed the National Assembly.
- The Bastille was stormed on 14 July 1789, becoming a symbol of resistance to royal authority.
- The Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen articulated liberty and equality before law.
- Feudal privileges were abolished in the revolutionary period.
- France became a republic in 1792; Louis XVI was executed in 1793.
- The Reign of Terror (1793–94) involved severe repression and executions under revolutionary authorities.
- The Directory governed after the Terror; Napoleon later rose to power.

## Ideas and impact
Liberty, equality and fraternity became key revolutionary ideals. The Revolution weakened feudal privilege and influenced political movements beyond France. Its history also shows how political instability and war can lead to violence and conflict over power.

## Important terms
Estate, privilege, monarchy, republic, constitution, suffrage, sovereignty, revolution.
