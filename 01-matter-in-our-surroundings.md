# Science — Matter in Our Surroundings
## What is matter?
Matter has mass and occupies space. Matter is made of tiny particles. These particles have spaces between them, are continuously moving, and attract one another.
Evidence: perfume spreads through a room (diffusion); sugar dissolves in water; gases can be compressed.

## States of matter
- Solid: fixed shape and volume; particles closely packed; generally least compressible.
- Liquid: fixed volume but takes container's shape; particles can slide past each other.
- Gas: no fixed shape or volume; particles far apart and highly compressible.
Changes of state: melting (solid→liquid), freezing (liquid→solid), vaporisation (liquid→gas), condensation (gas→liquid), sublimation (solid→gas directly), deposition (gas→solid).

## Temperature and latent heat
Temperature indicates how hot or cold a body is. During a change of state, heat may be absorbed or released without a temperature change; this energy is latent heat. Latent heat of fusion changes solid to liquid; latent heat of vaporisation changes liquid to gas.
Evaporation occurs at the surface at temperatures below boiling point. It increases with surface area, temperature, wind speed and lower humidity. Evaporation causes cooling because higher-energy particles escape.

## Celsius and Kelvin
K = °C + 273 (school-level approximation). Thus 25°C ≈ 298 K. Always use the conversion required by the question/textbook.

## Practice
1. Give three properties of particles of matter.
2. Why can gases be compressed more easily than solids?
3. Distinguish evaporation and boiling.
4. Why does evaporation cool a surface?
5. Convert 27°C to kelvin using K=°C+273.
6. Name the change from gas directly to solid.
## Answers
1. Particles have spaces, move continuously, and attract one another.
2. Gas particles have large spaces between them.
3. Evaporation is a surface phenomenon at many temperatures; boiling occurs throughout a liquid at its boiling point under specified pressure.
4. Higher-energy particles escape, lowering the average energy of those left behind.
5. 300 K.
6. Deposition.
