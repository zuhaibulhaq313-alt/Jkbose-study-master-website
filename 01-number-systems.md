# Mathematics — Chapter 1: Number Systems
## Learning goals
Understand natural, whole, integer, rational, irrational and real numbers; represent numbers on a number line; use laws of exponents; simplify surds.

## 1. Number sets
- Natural numbers: counting numbers, commonly {1, 2, 3, ...}. Some contexts include 0; follow your textbook's convention.
- Whole numbers: {0, 1, 2, 3, ...}.
- Integers: ..., −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...
- Rational number: can be written as p/q where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0. Its decimal expansion terminates or repeats.
- Irrational number: cannot be written as p/q; its decimal expansion is non-terminating and non-repeating. Examples include √2 and π.
- Real numbers: all rational and irrational numbers together.

## 2. Decimal expansions
A rational number in lowest terms has a terminating decimal exactly when its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5. Otherwise its decimal expansion repeats.
Examples: 3/8 = 0.375; 1/3 = 0.333...; 7/20 = 0.35.

## 3. Laws of exponents
For non-zero a and integers m,n:
- aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
- aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
- (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
- (ab)ᵐ = aᵐbᵐ
- (a/b)ᵐ = aᵐ/bᵐ, b ≠ 0
- a⁰ = 1; a⁻ᵐ = 1/aᵐ for a ≠ 0.

## 4. Surds and rationalisation
A surd is an irrational root expression such as √5. Simplify by extracting square factors: √72 = √(36×2) = 6√2.
To rationalise 1/√5, multiply top and bottom by √5: √5/5.
For 1/(√3+√2), multiply by the conjugate (√3−√2), giving √3−√2.

## Worked examples
1. Simplify √50 + √8 = 5√2 + 2√2 = 7√2.
2. Evaluate 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8.
3. Is 0.121212... rational? Yes, repeating decimals are rational.
4. Express 0.375 as a fraction: 375/1000 = 3/8.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to reduce p/q to lowest terms when checking decimal termination.
- Treating √(a+b) as √a + √b; this is generally false.
- Applying exponent laws when bases differ without first rewriting them.

## Practice
1. Classify −7, 0, 5/9, √7 and π.
2. Express 0.625 as a fraction in lowest terms.
3. Simplify √98 − √8.
4. Evaluate 3² × 3⁻⁴.
5. Rationalise 1/(√5−√2).
6. Explain why √3 is irrational in words, using the textbook's proof if required.
7. Which denominators give terminating decimals: 7/40, 13/30, 9/125?
## Answers
1. −7 integer/rational/real; 0 whole/integer/rational/real; 5/9 rational; √7 and π irrational real.
2. 5/8.
3. 7√2 − 2√2 = 5√2.
4. 3⁻² = 1/9.
5. (√5+√2)/3.
7. 7/40 and 9/125 terminate; 13/30 repeats.
## Quick revision
Real numbers = rational ∪ irrational. Rational decimals terminate or repeat. Check exponents carefully and simplify roots before combining.
