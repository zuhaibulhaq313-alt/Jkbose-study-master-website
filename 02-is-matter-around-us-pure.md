# Science — Is Matter Around Us Pure?
## Pure substances and mixtures
A pure substance contains one type of particle and has fixed composition: an element or compound. A mixture contains two or more substances physically combined and can have variable composition.
- Homogeneous mixture: uniform composition throughout, e.g. salt solution.
- Heterogeneous mixture: non-uniform composition, e.g. sand in water.
- Solution: solute + solvent; concentration may be described by mass-by-mass or mass-by-volume percentage.
- Suspension: particles are large enough to settle and can often be filtered.
- Colloid: particles do not settle readily and scatter light (Tyndall effect); examples include milk and fog.

## Separation techniques
- Filtration: insoluble solid from liquid.
- Evaporation/crystallisation: recover dissolved solid.
- Distillation: separate a solvent from a solution or liquids with suitable boiling-point differences.
- Separating funnel: immiscible liquids of different densities.
- Centrifugation: separates components based on density under spinning.
- Chromatography: separates dissolved components based on different movement through a medium.
Choose a method according to the physical properties of the components.

## Elements and compounds
Element: one kind of atom; cannot be decomposed chemically into simpler substances.
Compound: elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio; properties differ from constituent elements.
A mixture's components retain their individual properties and can often be separated physically.

## Practice
1. Classify air, distilled water, soil and copper as mixture/pure substance.
2. Which technique separates sand from water?
3. Why is milk called a colloid in school-level classification?
4. Give two differences between a compound and a mixture.
5. Which technique could separate coloured dyes in ink?
## Answers
1. Air mixture; distilled water pure compound; soil mixture; copper element.
2. Filtration.
3. Its dispersed particles do not settle readily and scatter light.
4. Compounds have fixed composition and chemical combination; mixtures have variable composition and components can often be physically separated.
5. Chromatography.
