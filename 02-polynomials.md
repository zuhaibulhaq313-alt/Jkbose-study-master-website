# Mathematics — Chapter 2: Polynomials
## Meaning and vocabulary
A polynomial in x is an expression made from terms aₙxⁿ where exponents are non-negative integers and coefficients are numbers. Examples: 4x²−3x+7; 5; x³+2x. Expressions such as 1/x and √x are not polynomials in x.
- Term: part separated by + or −.
- Coefficient: numerical factor of a term.
- Degree: greatest exponent with non-zero coefficient.
- Constant polynomial: degree 0 if non-zero; zero polynomial has special treatment and degree is often undefined.
- Zero of a polynomial: value k for which p(k)=0.

## Types by degree
Constant, linear (degree 1), quadratic (degree 2), cubic (degree 3), and higher-degree polynomials.

## Remainder and factor ideas
When a polynomial p(x) is divided by (x−a), the remainder is p(a). If p(a)=0, then (x−a) is a factor of p(x).
Useful identities:
(a+b)² = a²+2ab+b²
(a−b)² = a²−2ab+b²
a²−b² = (a−b)(a+b)
(x+a)(x+b)=x²+(a+b)x+ab
(a+b)³=a³+3a²b+3ab²+b³
(a−b)³=a³−3a²b+3ab²−b³
a³+b³=(a+b)(a²−ab+b²)
a³−b³=(a−b)(a²+ab+b²)

## Worked examples
1. Degree of 7x⁴−2x²+9 is 4.
2. For p(x)=x²−5x+6, p(2)=4−10+6=0, so 2 is a zero and (x−2) is a factor.
3. Factor x²−9 = (x−3)(x+3).
4. Expand (x+4)² = x²+8x+16.

## Practice
1. State the degree of 3x⁵−x+2.
2. Is 3/x a polynomial in x? Explain.
3. Find p(−1) for p(x)=2x²+3x−4.
4. Factorise x²+7x+12.
5. Expand (2x−3)².
6. Check whether x−1 is a factor of x³−1.
7. Use an identity to evaluate 102².
## Answers
1. 5.
2. No; x occurs with exponent −1.
3. 2−3−4 = −5.
4. (x+3)(x+4).
5. 4x²−12x+9.
6. p(1)=0, so yes.
7. (100+2)² = 10404.
## Exam method
Show substitution steps when finding a zero, and verify factorisation by multiplying factors back together.
