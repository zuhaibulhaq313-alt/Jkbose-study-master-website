# History — Socialism in Europe and the Russian Revolution
## Political ideas
Liberals emphasised individual rights and representative government; radicals sought wider political and social change; conservatives stressed tradition and gradual change. Socialists criticised unequal ownership and argued for social or collective control of major resources, though socialist groups differed in methods and goals.

## Russia before 1917
The Russian Empire was ruled by the Tsar. Many peasants faced hardship, industrial workers experienced difficult conditions, and political opposition was restricted. The 1905 Revolution followed unrest and the Bloody Sunday incident; the Tsar made concessions, including a Duma, but autocratic power persisted.

## 1917 revolutions
The February Revolution led to the Tsar's abdication and a Provisional Government. Soviets (councils of workers and soldiers) also held influence. The Bolsheviks, led by Lenin, promised peace, land and bread. In the October Revolution, Bolsheviks took power. Russia later experienced civil war and the formation of the USSR in 1922.

## Effects and cautions
The new government redistributed some land and nationalised major industries, while political power became concentrated under the Communist Party. The period involved civil war, coercion and major social transformation. Study both stated goals and historical consequences.
