# Mathematics — Chapter 3: Coordinate Geometry
## The Cartesian plane
Two perpendicular number lines form coordinate axes: horizontal x-axis and vertical y-axis. Their intersection is the origin O(0,0).
A point is written (x,y): x is the abscissa and y is the ordinate.
- Quadrant I: (+,+)
- Quadrant II: (−,+)
- Quadrant III: (−,−)
- Quadrant IV: (+,−)
Points on an axis are not in a quadrant.

## Plotting points
Start at the origin. Move horizontally according to x (right if positive, left if negative), then vertically according to y (up if positive, down if negative). Mark and label the point.
Examples: (−3,2) lies in Quadrant II; (4,−1) lies in Quadrant IV; (0,5) lies on the y-axis.

## Practice
1. Name the quadrant of (5,−2), (−4,−3), (−2,6).
2. What are the coordinates of the origin?
3. Where does (0,−7) lie?
4. Plot (2,3), (−2,3), (−2,−3), (2,−3) on graph paper.
5. Explain the difference between abscissa and ordinate.
## Answers
1. IV, III, II.
2. (0,0).
3. On the y-axis.
5. Abscissa is x-coordinate; ordinate is y-coordinate.
## Checklist
Label axes, show equal scales, mark the origin, and write ordered pairs with x first.
