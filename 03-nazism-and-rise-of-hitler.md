# History — Nazism and the Rise of Hitler
## Post-war Germany
Germany's defeat in World War I, the Treaty of Versailles, political instability, inflation and later the Great Depression weakened the Weimar Republic. Unemployment and public anger helped extremist parties gain support.

## Hitler's rise
The Nazi Party used propaganda, mass rallies and promises of national revival. Hitler became Chancellor in 1933. Democratic institutions were dismantled, opposition was suppressed, and the regime established a dictatorship. Racist and antisemitic ideology placed Jews and other targeted groups in danger.

## Nazi ideology and persecution
Nazism promoted racial hierarchy, aggressive nationalism, territorial expansion and obedience to a leader. The regime persecuted Jewish people, Roma, disabled people, political opponents and other groups. The Holocaust was the systematic genocide of six million Jews by Nazi Germany and its collaborators, alongside the persecution and murder of millions of other victims.

## Lessons
Propaganda, scapegoating, dehumanisation and the destruction of democratic checks can enable persecution. Historical study should recognise victims' humanity and the consequences of racism and dictatorship.
