# Science — Structure of the Atom
## Subatomic particles
- Electron: negative charge, very small mass, outside the nucleus in the school model.
- Proton: positive charge, mass about 1 u, in the nucleus.
- Neutron: no charge, mass about 1 u, in the nucleus.
Atomic number Z = number of protons. In a neutral atom, electrons = protons.
Mass number A = protons + neutrons. Neutrons = A−Z.

## Isotopes and isobars
Isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers (same element, different neutron count). Isobars have the same mass number but different atomic numbers.
Isotopes can have useful applications in medicine, industry and scientific research; always follow safe handling rules in real laboratories.

## Electronic arrangement
At this level, electrons are arranged in shells using the rules and maximum capacities specified in the textbook. For the first 18 elements, common school arrangements include Na: 2,8,1; Cl: 2,8,7; Ne: 2,8.
Valence electrons are electrons in the outermost shell and strongly affect chemical behaviour.

## Practice
1. An atom has Z=13 and A=27. Find protons, electrons (neutral atom), and neutrons.
2. Define isotope and isobar.
3. Write shell arrangement for oxygen (Z=8) and sodium (Z=11).
4. Why is an atom neutral when it has equal numbers of protons and electrons?
## Answers
1. 13 protons, 13 electrons, 14 neutrons.
2. Isotopes: same Z, different A; isobars: same A, different Z.
3. Oxygen 2,6; sodium 2,8,1.
4. Positive and negative charges cancel.
