# Science — The Fundamental Unit of Life
## Cell theory
The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Organisms may be unicellular or multicellular. New cells arise from pre-existing cells according to cell theory.

## Cell parts and functions
- Plasma membrane: selectively permeable boundary controlling exchange.
- Cell wall: rigid outer layer in plant cells; provides support and protection.
- Cytoplasm: site of many chemical reactions; contains organelles.
- Nucleus: contains genetic material and controls many cell activities.
- Mitochondria: release usable energy through cellular respiration; often called the powerhouse.
- Endoplasmic reticulum: synthesis and transport; rough ER has ribosomes, smooth ER helps lipid synthesis and detoxification.
- Golgi apparatus: modifies, packages and transports materials.
- Ribosomes: protein synthesis.
- Lysosomes: contain digestive enzymes.
- Vacuoles: storage; large central vacuole common in plant cells.
- Plastids: chloroplasts contain chlorophyll and carry out photosynthesis in plants.

## Plant and animal cells
Plant cells usually have a cell wall, plastids and a large central vacuole. Animal cells lack a cell wall and chloroplasts; vacuoles are usually smaller. These are general school-level distinctions.

## Diffusion and osmosis
Diffusion is net movement of particles from higher to lower concentration. Osmosis is movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration to lower water concentration. Cells may gain or lose water depending on the surrounding solution.

## Practice
1. Why is the cell called the basic unit of life?
2. Give two differences between plant and animal cells.
3. Match mitochondria, ribosome and Golgi apparatus to their functions.
4. Distinguish diffusion from osmosis.
5. What could happen to a plant cell placed in a very concentrated salt solution?
## Answers
1. Organisms are built from cells and life processes occur in cells.
2. Plant cells generally have a cell wall and plastids; animal cells do not.
3. Energy release; protein synthesis; modification/packaging/transport.
4. Osmosis specifically concerns water crossing a selectively permeable membrane.
5. Water may leave the cell, causing the protoplast to shrink away from the wall (plasmolysis).
