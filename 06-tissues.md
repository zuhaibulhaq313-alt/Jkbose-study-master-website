# Science — Tissues
## Definition
A tissue is a group of similar cells (and their products) working together to perform a specific function. Plants and animals have different tissue types suited to their needs.

## Plant tissues
Meristematic tissues divide actively:
- Apical meristem: growth in length at root and shoot tips.
- Intercalary meristem: growth at certain nodes or leaf bases.
- Lateral meristem: increase in thickness.
Permanent tissues arise from differentiation:
- Parenchyma: living, thin-walled cells; storage and photosynthesis when chloroplasts are present.
- Collenchyma: living cells with unevenly thickened walls; flexible support.
- Sclerenchyma: thick, lignified walls; strength, usually dead at maturity.
- Xylem: transports water and minerals and supports the plant.
- Phloem: transports sugars and other organic materials.

## Animal tissues
- Epithelial: covers surfaces and lines organs; protection, absorption and secretion.
- Connective: supports, binds and transports; includes bone, cartilage, blood, tendons and ligaments.
- Muscular: contraction and movement; skeletal, smooth and cardiac.
- Nervous: neurons transmit impulses; supporting cells help maintain nervous tissue.

## Practice
1. Compare xylem and phloem.
2. Which tissue helps a plant bend without breaking easily?
3. Name the four major animal tissue categories.
4. Distinguish tendon and ligament.
5. Why do meristematic cells divide actively?
## Answers
1. Xylem mainly moves water/minerals; phloem transports food substances.
2. Collenchyma.
3. Epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous.
4. Tendon connects muscle to bone; ligament connects bone to bone.
5. They produce new cells for growth.
