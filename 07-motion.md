# Science — Motion
## Distance and displacement
Distance is total path length (scalar). Displacement is the straight-line change from initial to final position with direction (vector). Distance is never negative; displacement magnitude cannot exceed distance.
Speed = distance/time. Velocity = displacement/time.
Average speed = total distance / total time.
Acceleration = change in velocity / time: a=(v−u)/t.

## Equations of uniformly accelerated motion
For constant acceleration:
v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as
where u is initial velocity, v final velocity, a acceleration, t time, s displacement. Use consistent units.

## Graphs
- Distance-time graph slope gives speed.
- Displacement-time graph slope gives velocity.
- Velocity-time graph slope gives acceleration; area under it gives displacement.
A horizontal position-time graph means the object is at rest.

## Worked example
A car starts from rest and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 s. u=0, a=2, t=5. v=u+at=10 m/s. s=ut+½at²=25 m.

## Practice
1. A runner travels 100 m in 20 s. Find average speed.
2. Velocity changes from 5 to 17 m/s in 4 s. Find acceleration.
3. A body starts from rest with a=3 m/s² for 4 s. Find final velocity and displacement.
4. Explain distance versus displacement.
5. What does the slope of a velocity-time graph represent?
## Answers
1. 5 m/s.
2. (17−5)/4=3 m/s².
3. v=12 m/s; s=½×3×16=24 m.
4. Distance is path length; displacement is directed change in position.
5. Acceleration.
