# Science — Force and Laws of Motion
## Force and inertia
Force is a push or pull that can change motion or shape. Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist a change in its state of rest or uniform straight-line motion. Mass measures inertia.
Newton's First Law: an object remains at rest or in uniform straight-line motion unless acted on by a net external force.
Second Law: net force is proportional to rate of change of momentum; for constant mass, F=ma.
Third Law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction; the forces act on different bodies.

## Momentum
Momentum p=mv. SI unit kg m/s. Impulse equals change in momentum and is related to force acting over time.
Conservation of momentum: total momentum of an isolated system remains constant if net external force is zero.

## Worked example
A 2 kg trolley accelerates at 3 m/s². Net force F=ma=2×3=6 N.

## Practice
1. State Newton's three laws in your own words.
2. Find momentum of a 4 kg object moving at 5 m/s.
3. Find force needed to accelerate 3 kg at 2 m/s².
4. Why do passengers tend to move forward when a moving bus stops suddenly?
5. Explain why action and reaction do not cancel each other on one object.
## Answers
2. 20 kg m/s.
3. 6 N.
4. Inertia of motion.
5. They act on different objects.
