# Science — Gravitation
## Universal gravitation
Every mass attracts every other mass. Newton's law: F = G m₁m₂/r², where r is separation between centres. G is the universal gravitational constant.
Weight W=mg, where g is gravitational acceleration. Mass is measured in kg and remains the same; weight is a force measured in newtons and may vary with g.
Near Earth's surface, use the value of g specified in the question (often about 9.8 m/s²).

## Free fall and buoyancy
An object in free fall is acted on by gravity alone (idealised model). Air resistance can change actual motion.
Pressure = force/area. Liquid pressure increases with depth.
Buoyant force is the upward force a fluid exerts on an immersed object.
Archimedes' principle: an immersed body experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced.
An object floats when buoyant force can balance its weight.

## Practice
1. Calculate the weight of a 5 kg object when g=10 m/s².
2. State two differences between mass and weight.
3. What is buoyancy?
4. State Archimedes' principle.
5. Why can a large ship float while a small metal nail sinks?
## Answers
1. 50 N.
2. Mass is amount of matter/inertia and measured in kg; weight is gravitational force measured in N.
3. Upward force exerted by a fluid.
5. The ship's overall shape displaces enough water to provide buoyancy equal to its weight; the nail does not.
