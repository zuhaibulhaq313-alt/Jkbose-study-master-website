# Science — Sound
## Production and propagation
Sound is produced by vibrating objects and travels through a medium as a mechanical wave. It cannot travel through a vacuum. In air, sound is typically longitudinal: compressions and rarefactions travel through the medium.
Wave speed v = frequency f × wavelength λ. Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz); wavelength in metres; speed in m/s.
Time period T=1/f. Greater frequency means higher pitch; greater amplitude generally means louder sound.

## Reflection and hearing
Echo is reflected sound heard separately when the delay is sufficient. Reverberation is persistence of sound due to repeated reflections. Absorbent materials can reduce reverberation.
The human audible range is commonly approximated as 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, though it varies by person and age. Ultrasound is above this range; infrasound is below it.
The ear receives sound; the eardrum vibrates, and signals are transmitted through the hearing system to the brain.
